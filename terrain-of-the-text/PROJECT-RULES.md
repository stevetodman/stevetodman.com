# Project Rules — Terrain of the Text

These rules are non-negotiable unless this file is deliberately revised in an approved round.

## 1. Accuracy outranks appearance

If a beautiful representation conflicts with the historical, geographic, textual, or archaeological evidence, the representation loses.

## 2. Never silently increase certainty

Do not turn `contested` into `probable`, or `probable` into `secure`, without documenting the evidence and the decision.

Do not convert an interpretive proposal into a geographic fact.

## 3. Separate kinds of confidence

A place identification, route reconstruction, date, historical claim, and exegetical interpretation may have different confidence levels. Do not collapse them into one confidence score.

## 4. Provenance is required for map-changing facts

Every coordinate, route geometry, elevation profile, ancient road, and contested identification that reaches the atlas must be traceable to a named source.

Ordinary uncontested facts may carry citations inline. Contested claims belong in `evidence/CONTESTED-CLAIMS.md`.

## 5. Rejected claims stay rejected unless new evidence is documented

Before adding a familiar Bible-map assertion, search `REJECTED-CLAIMS.md`. A rejected assertion may return only with a documented reason and evidence.

## 6. Do not invent precision

No arbitrary candidate weights, exact travel times, exact route lengths, exact dates, or small uncertainty radii merely because the interface can display them.

Use ranges or qualitative language where the evidence only supports ranges or qualitative conclusions.

## 7. Unknown is not a point

If a location is genuinely unknown, do not place a precise marker at an arbitrary centroid and let it look like a site. Represent the known region or search area explicitly.

## 8. Routes are evidence, not straight lines

A route line must reflect the best available evidence for terrain, roads, passes, water crossings, and period-appropriate movement. Uncertain segments are shown as uncertain.

## 9. No anachronism by default

Modern national borders, modern political labels, and modern city prominence do not belong in the default biblical-period map unless they are necessary for orientation and clearly separated from the historical layer.

## 10. Preserve approved behavior

Each round has a narrow scope. Do not silently change approved coordinates, labels, confidence levels, interaction behavior, print behavior, accessibility, or visual language outside that scope.

## 11. One episode at a time after the calibration episode

Do not bulk-generate the remaining corpus. Each episode must earn its place by materially changing how the geographic setting is understood.

## 12. Main means approved

In the permanent repository, `main` must always be the last approved state. Work happens on a round branch and is merged only after the single round audit passes.

## 13. Git tags are the snapshots

Do not maintain duplicate HTML archives or a separate changelog. Tag approved milestones.

## 14. The product is explanatory, not devotional or sensational

Avoid invented drama, pious filler, cinematic language, and claims that geography proves a theological interpretation. State what the terrain, texts, sources, and uncertainty actually support.
