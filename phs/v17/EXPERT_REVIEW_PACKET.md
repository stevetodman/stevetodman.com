# Pediatric Hospital Simulator — Expert Review Packet

## Release under review

- Case: **Night Shift: The Closing Window**
- Platform release: **v1.7**
- Case schema version: **1.0**
- Intended use: formative pediatric resident education
- Explicit boundary: this simulation does **not** establish competence, entrustment, certification, or readiness for independent practice.

## Review purpose

This packet supports structured review by pediatric cardiology, critical care, hospital medicine, infectious disease, simulation education, assessment, nursing, and patient/family communication experts.

Reviewers should assess:

1. clinical accuracy and safety;
2. plausibility of time course and treatment response;
3. alignment between objectives, scenario events, rubric, and debrief;
4. acceptable alternative pathways;
5. unintended cueing or gaming;
6. fairness across training levels;
7. clarity of the validity claim boundary.

## Learning objectives

1. Prioritize competing pediatric patients and reallocate attention when new acuity signals appear.
2. Recognize neonatal shock despite inherited framing, seek disconfirming evidence, and revise the working diagnosis.
3. Treat time-critical ductal-dependent systemic circulation while maintaining parallel coverage of reversible neonatal threats.
4. Reassess after intervention and close the loop from order placement through review, interpretation, action, or safe handoff.
5. Escalate, delegate, and communicate using graded urgency and closed-loop confirmation.
6. Transfer illness severity, active problems, actions, pending work, contingencies, and ownership using structured I-PASS.

## Scenario blueprint

### Primary case: Maya Carter

- Six-day-old term infant
- Progressive poor feeding, tachypnea, oliguria, and lower-extremity hypoperfusion
- Misleading or incomplete inherited framing varies by case version
- Underlying truth: critical coarctation with ductal-dependent systemic circulation
- Parallel threats retained in the initial management model: sepsis, hypoglycemia, metabolic disease, myocarditis
- Deterministic deterioration window is an educational construct, not a claim of universal real-world timing

### Competing patients

- Eli: bronchiolitis with evolving hypoxemia
- Nora: febrile young infant with staged blood-culture uncertainty and eventual GBS identification
- Jamal: low-risk chest pain with caregiver pressure for low-value escalation

## Critical actions currently modeled

- Recognize differential perfusion and shock physiology
- Monitor and establish vascular access
- Check point-of-care glucose
- Obtain blood culture and begin empiric neonatal sepsis coverage
- Prepare airway and respiratory support
- Start prostaglandin before severe deterioration
- Escalate using closed-loop communication
- Reassess after treatment
- Review and interpret critical results
- Transfer ownership of pending work

## Acceptable alternative pathways requiring reviewer input

Reviewers should identify whether the following should receive full, partial, or no credit:

- starting prostaglandin before echocardiographic confirmation based on bedside shock pattern;
- obtaining gas/lactate before versus after initial treatment;
- cautious fluid aliquot with immediate reassessment;
- antibiotic timing relative to cultures when vascular access is delayed;
- sequencing of cardiology, critical care, and attending escalation;
- lumbar puncture timing in Nora after preliminary culture versus definitive GBS identification;
- escalation from low-flow oxygen to high-flow support in Eli based on trajectory.

## Rubric review questions

For every rubric item, reviewers should answer:

1. Is the behavior observable in the simulator?
2. Is the behavior clinically necessary, optional, or context-dependent?
3. Is the point weight proportionate to educational importance?
4. Are there acceptable alternatives not recognized by the engine?
5. Could the item reward unsafe or low-value care?
6. Is the rationale suitable for a learner-facing debrief?
7. Is the stated consequence plausible and appropriately qualified?

## Proposed standard-setting pathway

The current minimum is provisional and formative.

Before consequential use:

1. Conduct independent expert review of content and rubric.
2. Revise the rubric using documented consensus.
3. Perform response-process testing with representative learners.
4. Use a defensible standard-setting method such as Mastery Angoff or a documented patient-safety standard.
5. Test known-groups performance across training levels.
6. Evaluate reliability and sensitivity to gaming.
7. Compare simulation evidence with external workplace-based assessment data.
8. Monitor unintended consequences and subgroup fairness.
